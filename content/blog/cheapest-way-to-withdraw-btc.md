Withdrawing Bitcoin from an exchange sounds simple, but the fees can vary dramatically depending on which exchange you use and which network you select. In this guide, we compare BTC withdrawal costs across the top 5 exchanges and reveal the cheapest options.

## Why Bitcoin Withdrawal Fees Vary

Bitcoin withdrawal fees are set by the exchange (not the Bitcoin network directly). Each exchange adds a markup on top of the actual network transaction fee. Some exchanges update their fees frequently based on network congestion, while others use a fixed rate.

The Bitcoin network itself has variable fees depending on mempool congestion. During busy periods, a simple BTC transaction can cost $5–$20 on-chain.

## BTC Withdrawal Fee Comparison (2026)

- **Binance:** 0.00001 BTC (~$0.67) via Lightning Network, 0.0002 BTC (~$13.50) via on-chain
- **Kraken:** 0.00015 BTC (~$10.12) via on-chain
- **Coinbase:** Dynamic fee (usually $1–$5 via on-chain)
- **OKX:** 0.0001 BTC (~$6.75) via on-chain, supports Lightning
- **KuCoin:** 0.0005 BTC (~$33.75) via on-chain

**Cheapest option: Binance via Lightning Network** at under $1.

## The Lightning Network Advantage

If your destination supports the Lightning Network, you can transfer BTC for less than $0.01 in most cases. The catch is that not all exchanges or wallets support Lightning. Currently supported on:

- Binance (send and receive)
- OKX (send and receive)
- Kraken (send and receive)
- Coinbase (limited support)

## Alternative Strategy: Convert and Transfer

If Lightning is not available, consider this workaround:

1. Convert BTC to XRP or SOL on your exchange
2. Withdraw the XRP/SOL (fees under $0.25)
3. Deposit on the destination exchange
4. Convert back to BTC

This roundabout method can save you $10–$30 compared to a direct BTC on-chain transfer, though you take on brief price risk during the conversion.
